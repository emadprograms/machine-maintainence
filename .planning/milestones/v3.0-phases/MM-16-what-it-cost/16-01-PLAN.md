# Phase 16 — What it cost: hours, money, and one month of it

## Goal

A finished job was recorded as a job that happened, not as work that took four hours
and consumed a bearing. This phase adds the two numbers that turn a history into a
record, and a screen that adds them up for a month.

## Decisions

* **Hours are nullable.** A job nobody has costed is not a job that took zero hours.
  The column is `REAL NULL`, and the list shows `—` rather than `0`.
* **One rate, stored in the database.** A `settings` table with a single row beats a
  constant in the source: changing it is a form, not a deploy, and the report can say
  which rate produced its numbers.
* **Parts cost comes from the parts table** (`qty × unit_price` at the time of the
  report). Labour is `hours × rate`. Nothing cleverer — per-person rates and overtime
  are a different product.
* **Cost is computed on the server**, once, and returned as `cost` on each work order.
  A number the database can compute should not be recomputed in the browser, where
  nothing tests it.
* **Months are half-open ranges** (`2026-11-01` to `2026-12-01`), so no job can land in
  two months and February needs no special case.
* **The report is a screen, not a dialog.** It has its own export, its own empty state
  and its own error state, following the pattern of every screen since v2.0.

## Proof

Fourteen new assertions (226 → 240) and two mutations:

| Defect | Caught by |
|---|---|
| Negative hours accepted; anyone may set the rate | `negative hours are refused in words` (stored `-2`), `only an admin can change the hourly rate` (status 200) |
| The list cost ignores the configured rate | `a job costs parts plus hours at the configured rate` — cost 25, expected 125 |
